- **作者**：Xue He, Hans Gløckner Giil, Caiqiong Xu, Jicheng Wang, Arne Brataas, Jinbo Yang 等

> Abstract Magnetoresistive effects are typically symmetric under magnetization reversal. However, nonlinear spin transport can give rise to unidirectional magnetoresistance in systems with strong spin–orbit interaction and broken inversion symmetry. Here, we report a large magnonic unidirectional spin Hall magnetoresistance (USMR) in a van der Waals antiferromagnet CrPS4 in contact with Pt, driven by the nonequilibrium magnon accumulation with a finite magnon chemical potential. In contrast to conventional magnonic USMR driven by magnetization fluctuations, this effect persists under strong magnetic fields and low temperatures. The magnitude of magnonic USMR in CrPS4/Pt exceeds that of YIG/Pt by more than 2 orders of magnitude and surpasses the electrical USMR in metallic Ta/Co bilayers by a factor of 2. Our results highlight magnon chemical potential gradients for nonlinear magnetoresistance in antiferromagnetic van der Waals heterostructures and open a route to magnon-based antiferromagnetic spintronic functionalities in two-terminal device geometries.

> This work investigates the nature of the ground state of the antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model on fundamental kagome cells, a triangle and a star, using the variational quantum eigensolver algorithm on real quantum hardware. Our custom ansatz is capable of accurately recovering meaningful properties of the ground state such as the spin-spin correlation terms and static structure factor without explicit error mitigation. > Abstract Iron-based ferromagnetic materials have emerged as a promising system for spintronics due to their high Curie temperatures and the significant anomalous Hall effect (AHE) driven by Berry curvature. While there have been investigations into the epitaxial growth of nonlayered iron-based ferromagnetic materials, the realization of high-quality single crystal thin films continues to be a formidable obstacle. Meanwhile, issues such as grain boundary scattering further hinder their characterization and application. Here, we report the first chemical vapor deposition synthesis of single-crystalline cubic D03-Fe3Ge nanoplates. This compound represents a substantial departure from the conventional hexagonal phases typically observed in the Fe–Ge system. Structural characterizations confirm the cubic lattice and the stoichiometric purity of the crystals. Structural analysis identifies a strong epitaxial effect, resulting in a giant compressive strain of ∼4.16% at the interface that relaxes gradually toward the surface. The second-harmonic generation signals might further reveal that such interfacial strain disrupted the inherent centrosymmetry of the cubic phase. Superconducting quantum interference device magnetometry shows robust room-temperature ferromagnetism and clear magnetic anisotropy. Magneto-transport measurements reveal a robust AHE that remains stable at temperatures far exceeding the room temperature. The scaling observed between the anomalous Hall resistance and longitudinal resistance strongly suggests that this effect is dominated by the intrinsic Berry curvature mechanism. Furthermore, density functional theory calculations reveal that the huge epitaxial strain induces band reconstructions and creates dense spin–orbit coupling gaps near the Fermi level, effectively doubling the anomalous Hall conductivity to match our experimental observations.

> Surface spin waves of the Damon-Eshbach type exhibit intrinsically nonreciprocal transport properties with a chiral dynamical field structure that localizes counterpropagating waves at opposite film surfaces. Such chirality has been predicted to suppress direct backscattering in thin films within frequency ranges free of bulk modes. However, how chirality influences reflection in thicker three-dimensional magnetic media, where a dense spectrum of bulk excitations overlaps with surface waves, remains unclear. Here we demonstrate that, in micrometer-thick yttrium iron garnet films, reflection of the chiral Damon-Eshbach wave from the boundary of the magnetic medium is accompanied by excitation of spatially localized thickness-quantized bulk modes, whereas reciprocal backward-volume waves reflect nearly elastically. Brillouin light scattering spectroscopy, infrared thermography, and micromagnetic simulations reveal standing bulk excitations at the reflecting boundary and quantify the associated magnon energy accumulation and dissipation. These results identify bulk-mode excitations as the physical pathway enabling reversal of chirally localized surface waves in thick films, thereby defining the limits of chirality-based backscattering immunity and providing a general framework for wave transport in nonreciprocal magnetic media.
